## What this override does not cover

The defect it fixes is real and independently confirmed. The risk is not that
the fix is unnecessary — it is that a calculation change with this little
outside scrutiny can be right in its diagnosis and still wrong in a detail
nobody checked.

- **The named authority was never consulted.** DOCTRINE §3 names the Hong
  Kong Observatory tables as the calibration source for exactly these values.
  `hko.gov.hk` is blocked by the implementing environment's egress policy
  (403 on CONNECT at the proxy; its README forbids retrying policy denials).
  Every number here rests on substitutes. **Re-confirm 1916, 1954, 2027 and
  2030 against HKO from an environment that can reach it** — this is the
  single most valuable follow-up attached to this file.

- **"Three independent implementations" overstates the independence.**
  `sxtwl` is an astronomical implementation and genuinely independent.
  `lunardate` and `borax` are both table-based and may share a table lineage;
  their perfect agreement is consistent with independence *or* with common
  ancestry, and this lane did not establish which. For **1916** — the year
  actually changed — ICU agrees too, giving three distinguishable lineages
  (ICU, sxtwl, the table family) and making it the best-supported of the
  four. The three years left unchanged (1954, 2027, 2030) rest on **fewer**
  lineages, since ICU is the outlier there. The change is better evidenced
  than the decisions not to change.

- **The 1929 threshold is asserted, not derived.** Some implementations place
  the China Standard Time adoption at 1928. It is immaterial to the outputs —
  no offset-sensitive date in 1900–2100 falls in 1928, so the table is
  identical either way — but the constant in the source is stated on this
  lane's authority and was not checked against a primary source.

- **The three solar-term changes are unpinned by fixtures.** 1911, 1912 and
  1927 move, and no fixture covers them; they are constrained only by the
  jieqi-window test, which passes under both the old and the new values.
  A future regression on those three would be silent.

- **What is genuinely reassuring, so the balance is honest.** The change is
  two lines and trivially revertible; `tests/fixtures.json` now pins the new
  boundary in both directions (`1916-02-02 → rabbit`, `1916-02-03 → dragon`);
  `tests/calendar.test.js` pins `1916 → [2, 3]` explicitly so a silent return
  to Feb 4 fails loudly; and the corrected implementation matches every
  oracle on every year 1900–2100, where the previous one did not. The
  blast radius was measured across all 201 years rather than estimated.

- **Scope:** this override covers **#140 only**.
